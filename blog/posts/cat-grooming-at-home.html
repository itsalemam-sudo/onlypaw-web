Grooming a cat at home without losing skin (yours or theirs)

Published June 2026 · 5 min read · Filed in Grooming

Cats groom themselves — until they can't. Long-coats mat, seniors stop reaching their lower back, and every cat sheds enough in shedding season to build a second cat. Here's the at-home routine that keeps most cats out of the professional groomer for everything but the annual full clip.

How often to brush, by coat type

  • Shorthair (Domestic, Siamese, Burmese): once a week, 3 minutes.
  • Medium coat (British Shorthair, Ragdoll juveniles): twice a week, 5 minutes.
  • Longhair (Persian, Maine Coon, adult Ragdoll): daily, 5–10 minutes. Skipping two days can start a mat.

The tools worth owning

  1. A slicker brush for the coat body.
  2. A fine metal comb for behind the ears, armpits, and belly — the four mat hotspots.
  3. Cat-specific nail clippers — human clippers crush the nail sideways and split it.
  4. Styptic powder for the one time you nick the quick. You will, eventually.

The claw trim, without a wrestling match

  1. Wait until the cat is already relaxed — post-nap, lying on your lap.
  2. Hold one paw. Press gently on the pad; the claw extends.
  3. Look for the pink triangle inside — that's the quick, and it bleeds if you cut it. Trim only the clear tip beyond it.
  4. One nail is a win. Reward with a treat. Do the next paw tomorrow. There is no rule that says all 18 claws come off in one session.

Ears and eyes

Weekly, glance inside the ears. Pale pink is normal. Dark brown crumbly wax is ear mites — vet trip. A yeasty smell is infection — vet trip. Never stick a cotton bud into the canal; wipe only the visible flap with a soft cloth and warm water.

Some breeds (Persians, Exotics) have tear staining under the eyes. Wipe daily with a soft cloth — leaving it invites a skin infection.

Skin signs that mean stop and call a vet

  • Bald patches with pink scaly skin (ringworm — contagious to humans).
  • Scabs along the spine (flea allergy dermatitis; treat fleas even if you don't see any).
  • A single lump that wasn't there last week (any age; not "just old age").
  • Sudden change in grooming habit — over-grooming a spot bald usually means pain underneath.

When to book a professional

Book a groomer when: a mat is bigger than a coin (cutting it out at home risks slicing skin), your cat's rear becomes soiled and can't self-clean, or annually for long-coats who need a lion-clip in summer.
