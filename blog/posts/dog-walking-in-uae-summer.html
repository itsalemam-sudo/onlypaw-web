Walking your dog through a UAE summer without overheating them

Published June 2026 · 5 min read · Filed in Walking

From mid-May to October, Dubai pavements regularly exceed 60°C in direct sun. A simple test: press the back of your hand to the pavement for five seconds. If you can't hold it, your dog's pads can't either — and they'll burn before they show pain.

1. The temperature window

Aim for walks before 7am or after 8pm during summer months. By 7am the surface has had a chance to cool overnight. After 8pm direct sun is gone and surfaces drop fast.

2. Surface, not just air, temperature

Asphalt can be 25°C hotter than the air. Concrete tiles in shaded community gardens stay much cooler. Grass is cooler again. Where possible, walk on grass; sand burns badly, so beach walks need water-shoes or pre-soaked sand.

3. Hydration that actually works

  • Carry a collapsible bowl. Don't expect them to drink from a sports-bottle nozzle.
  • Offer water every 10 minutes, not just at the end.
  • A frozen wet bandanna around the neck cools the carotid blood-flow — humans use the same trick.

4. Breeds that need shorter walks

Brachycephalic (short-nosed) breeds — Bulldogs, Pugs, French Bulldogs, Pekingese — can't pant efficiently. A 15-minute outdoor walk in 35°C heat can be fatal. In summer, indoor enrichment + 5-minute potty breaks is safer than longer outings.

5. Heatstroke signs to recognise

Heavy panting, drooling, bright red gums, stumbling, vomiting. If you see any of these, get them into shade immediately, wet their belly and paws with cool (not ice) water, and call a vet. Heatstroke kills in under an hour without intervention.
