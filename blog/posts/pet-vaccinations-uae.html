Pet vaccinations every UAE owner should keep current

Published June 2026 · 6 min read · Filed in Health

This post is general information, not veterinary advice — always confirm the exact schedule with your own vet.

Dubai Municipality and Abu Dhabi Agriculture and Food Safety Authority both require certain vaccinations for dogs and cats registered in the emirate. Beyond the legal minimum, there's a second layer most UAE vets recommend — especially for pets that board, use dog parks, or travel to other emirates.

Dogs — core (required)

  • Rabies — legally required. First shot at 12–16 weeks, then annual booster in the UAE (some countries move to 3-year, but UAE law is annual).
  • DHPP — Distemper, Hepatitis, Parainfluenza, Parvovirus. Puppy series: 6–8 weeks, 10–12 weeks, 14–16 weeks. Adult booster every 3 years (or annual — check with your vet).

Dogs — non-core (recommended if the pet socialises)

  • Bordetella (kennel cough). Required by every reputable boarding facility. Every 6–12 months.
  • Leptospirosis. Not universal in the UAE, but relevant if you hike in wadis or travel to greener emirates. Annual.
  • Canine influenza. Talk to your vet — outbreak-driven.

Cats — core (required)

  • Rabies — same rule as dogs; legally required if registered.
  • FVRCP — Feline Viral Rhinotracheitis, Calicivirus, Panleukopenia. Kitten series: 6–8 weeks, 10–12 weeks, 14–16 weeks. Adult booster every 3 years.

Cats — non-core

  • FeLV (feline leukemia). Recommended for outdoor cats or multi-cat households. Annual.
  • Chlamydia felis. Only if there's local outbreak pressure.

What to keep in the folder

Boarding hosts on OnlyPaw ask for a scan or photo of the vaccination card before drop-off. Keep a PDF on your phone: last rabies date, DHPP/FVRCP dates, and the vet clinic stamp on each. Kennels legally can't accept a pet without it — don't turn up hoping to negotiate.
