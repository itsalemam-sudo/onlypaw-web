What to look for when you book a pet taxi

Published June 2026 · 4 min read · Filed in Travel

Vet appointment. Boarding drop-off. A move across town. If you don't own a car — or can't fit a large-breed crate in the one you have — a pet taxi solves it. But "pet taxi" is not a licensed category in most UAE emirates; the quality range is enormous. Here's the checklist we run through before booking.

1. Restraint: crate over harness over nothing

An unrestrained pet in a car is unsafe for the pet and the driver — a 20kg dog in a 50 km/h impact hits the seat in front with the force of a 500kg missile. A hard-sided crate, strapped to the seatbelt, is the safest option. A crash-tested harness is acceptable for calm dogs. A leash clipped to a headrest is not — it does not restrain, and can strangle a dog thrown forward on braking.

2. Vehicle temperature

Ask what the cabin temperature is set to. In UAE summer, the answer should be 20–22°C at pick-up so the pet enters an already-cool space. If a driver says "I turn on the A/C when they get in," pick someone else — that's 5 minutes of a hot metal box for your pet.

3. Driver experience with your species and size

A driver who's confident with medium dogs may not know how to hold a bolt-happy cat carrier. Ask directly: "How many cats/large dogs/reactive dogs have you transported this month?" Real answers sound specific ("three this week, one Persian yesterday"); vague answers ("oh, lots") are a red flag.

4. Live tracking and photos

Every pet taxi ride on OnlyPaw shares a live location link from pick-up to drop-off, and the driver sends a photo confirmation on both ends. If you're booking off-platform, ask the driver for a WhatsApp location share. If they refuse, don't book.

5. Insurance and paperwork

Ask whether the provider carries liability insurance covering the animal in transit. Most consumer car policies specifically exclude injury to a pet passenger. On OnlyPaw, all provider pet taxi rides include in-transit cover as standard.

Red flags — don't book

  • "I put them in the boot" — never for cars without a boot-to-cabin airflow system.
  • No crate, no harness, no plan.
  • Won't share a photo of the vehicle interior.
  • Willing to transport multiple unrelated pets in the same crate.

Before the ride

  • Skip the meal 3 hours before pick-up — carsickness is worse on a full stomach.
  • Walk / potty break just before departure.
  • Familiar blanket in the crate.
  • Vaccination card scanned to your phone if you're heading to a boarding facility.
