Pet Sitting in Singapore, Singapore

Typical 2026 range · S$33.5–S$56 per drop-in visit · Vetted providers on OnlyPaw

Pet Sitting looks different in Singapore than it does in a stock photo. The city's tropical rainforest, hot and humid year-round sets the rhythm — hot months (Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec) push serious walkers into the morning and late-evening slots, and cooler shoulders reopen the map. West Coast Park is on almost every regular route around here. NParks maintains designated Dogs-Off-Leash Areas — outside them, leashes are mandatory. That local nuance is the difference between a generic booking and one your pet actually looks forward to.

What a good pet sitter in Singapore looks like

  • Adjusts schedule around Singapore's hot months (Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec) rather than forcing a fixed slot.
  • Can name and use at least two routes through East Coast Park, not just the nearest sidewalk.
  • Comfortable with your building's access rules (fobs, gates, doormen) on day one.
  • Reads paw-pad temperature by hand on hot surfaces before letting your dog step off the grass.
  • Carries water and a collapsible bowl on every pet-sitting visit in Singapore — non-negotiable in this climate.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pet sitting typically cost in Singapore?

Typical 2026 rates range S$33.5–S$56 per drop-in visit. Ranges reflect duration, day of week, and how the provider structures their packages.

When are the best hours to book pet sitting in Singapore?

Given Singapore's tropical rainforest, hot and humid year-round, the hot months (Jan, Feb, Mar, Apr, May, Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep, Oct, Nov, Dec) push scheduled visits into cooler pockets — early morning and after sunset are the standard. Shoulder months open the day up.

What should I ask a pet sitter on their first visit in Singapore?

Ask which routes they use in your neighborhood, how they read heat, cold, or weather stress in a dog, and how they'd handle an emergency. A Singapore pro can name three routes and one nearest 24-hour vet without hesitation.

Are group walks common in Singapore?

Yes — small-group walks are widely available, priced below solo, and work well for social dogs. If your dog is reactive or recovering from injury, book solo and say why upfront.

A Singapore provider working on OnlyPaw is briefed on this. Bring it up before the first visit so you're both aligned on route choice and timing.
