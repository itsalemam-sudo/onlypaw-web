What a good cat sitter in Copenhagen looks like

  • Reads paw-pad temperature by hand on hot surfaces before letting your dog step off the grass.
  • Carries water and a collapsible bowl on every cat-sitting visit in Copenhagen — non-negotiable in this climate.
  • Comfortable with your building's access rules (fobs, gates, doormen) on day one.
  • Handles multi-pet households without stacking too many dogs on one leash.
  • Can name and use at least two routes through Amager Fælled, not just the nearest sidewalk.

Frequently asked questions

How much does cat sitting typically cost in Copenhagen?

Typical 2026 rates range 137 kr–240 kr per visit. Ranges reflect duration, day of week, and how the provider structures their packages.

When are the best hours to book cat sitting in Copenhagen?

Given Copenhagen's temperate oceanic, cool, the hot months (Jul, Aug) push scheduled visits into cooler pockets — early morning and after sunset are the standard. Shoulder months open the day up.

What should I ask a cat sitter on their first visit in Copenhagen?

Ask which routes they use in your neighborhood, how they read heat, cold, or weather stress in a dog, and how they'd handle an emergency. A Copenhagen pro can name three routes and one nearest 24-hour vet without hesitation.

Are group walks common in Copenhagen?

Yes — small-group walks are widely available, priced below solo, and work well for social dogs. If your dog is reactive or recovering from injury, book solo and say why upfront.

Danish city parks generally require dogs on-lead unless the area is a marked hundeskov (dog forest) — how does that affect my booking?

A Copenhagen provider working on OnlyPaw is briefed on this. Bring it up before the first visit so you're both aligned on route choice and timing.
