Pet Boarding in Paris, France

Typical 2026 range · €41.4–€83 per night · Vetted providers on OnlyPaw

Pet Boarding looks different in Paris than it does in a stock photo. The city's temperate oceanic sets the rhythm — hot months (Jul, Aug) push serious walkers into the morning and late-evening slots, and cooler shoulders reopen the map. Bois de Boulogne is on almost every regular route around here. Many central Parisian parks require dogs on-lead — the Bois de Vincennes has fenced off-lead zones. That local nuance is the difference between a generic booking and one your pet actually looks forward to.

What a good pet boarder in Paris looks like

  • Handles multi-pet households without stacking too many dogs on one leash.
  • Comfortable with your building's access rules (fobs, gates, doormen) on day one.
  • Carries water and a collapsible bowl on every boarding night in Paris — non-negotiable in this climate.
  • Reads paw-pad temperature by hand on hot surfaces before letting your dog step off the grass.
  • Can name and use at least two routes through Parc de la Villette, not just the nearest sidewalk.

Frequently asked questions

How much does pet boarding typically cost in Paris?

Typical 2026 rates range €41.4–€83 per night. Ranges reflect duration, day of week, and how the provider structures their packages.

When are the best hours to book pet boarding in Paris?

Given Paris's temperate oceanic, the hot months (Jul, Aug) push scheduled visits into cooler pockets — early morning and after sunset are the standard. Shoulder months open the day up.

What should I ask a pet boarder on their first visit in Paris?

Ask which routes they use in your neighborhood, how they read heat, cold, or weather stress in a dog, and how they'd handle an emergency. A Paris pro can name three routes and one nearest 24-hour vet without hesitation.

Are group walks common in Paris?

Yes — small-group walks are widely available, priced below solo, and work well for social dogs. If your dog is reactive or recovering from injury, book solo and say why upfront.

Many central Parisian parks require dogs on-lead — the Bois de Vincennes has fenced off-lead zones — how does that affect my booking?

A Paris provider working on OnlyPaw is briefed on this. Bring it up before the first visit so you're both aligned on route choice and timing.
